The afternoon slump without coffee: why it happens and what may help

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

Many people feel a dip in the mid-afternoon whether or not they drink coffee: research describes a “post-lunch dip” linked to a rise in sleepiness at that time of day, and it can happen even without lunch.⁠[1] Short naps and a few minutes of movement have both been studied as ways to feel more alert.⁠[2]⁠[3]

Why does the afternoon slump happen?

A review of the research describes the post-lunch dip as a real effect with its roots in human biology, possibly linked to the body clock, and not only to what you ate.⁠[1] It also found the dip is made worse by a high-carbohydrate lunch and may be more likely in strong “morning people”.⁠[1]

Is it worse after quitting caffeine?

It may be for a while. Fatigue, drowsiness and decreased alertness are confirmed caffeine withdrawal symptoms, which typically last 2–9 days.⁠[4] A study of 379 people found that in regular caffeine users, going without caffeine lowered alertness, and having caffeine mostly brought it back to normal rather than above it.⁠[5] So some people notice the afternoon dip more in the first days without coffee. See tiredness after quitting caffeine.

What may help without coffee

  • A short nap. In a study of 24 young adults who had slept about 5 hours, a 10-minute nap at 3 pm improved sleepiness, fatigue, vigour and performance straight away, with some benefits lasting up to 155 minutes.⁠[2] A 30-minute nap left people groggy at first before they improved.⁠[2]
  • A few minutes of movement. In a small study of 18 young women short on sleep, 10 minutes of stair walking left them feeling more energetic than a 50 mg caffeine capsule, though the lift was short-lived.⁠[3]
  • A lighter lunch. The post-lunch dip is made worse by a high-carbohydrate lunch,⁠[1] so some people find a lighter or more balanced midday meal helps.
  • A caffeine-free drink for the ritual. If the 3 pm coffee is a habit as much as a pick-me-up, a herbal tea keeps the break without caffeine. See coffee swaps.

If you still have an afternoon coffee

Caffeine lasts longer than many people expect. Its mean half-life in healthy people is about 5 hours, with a range of 1.5 to 9.5 hours.⁠[6] The authors of a study on caffeine and sleep recommend avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bedtime.⁠[7] The wear-off calculator estimates how much of an afternoon cup may still be in your body at bedtime, and caffeine and sleep has more.

Questions

Why do I get tired in the afternoon?

Many people do. Research describes a mid-afternoon “post-lunch dip” linked to a rise in sleepiness at that time of day, which can happen even without lunch.⁠[1]

How long should a nap be for the afternoon slump?

In one study of adults short on sleep, a 10-minute nap gave the best overall results; a 30-minute nap left people groggy at first.⁠[2]

Does a walk help more than coffee?

In one small study of young women short on sleep, 10 minutes of stair walking felt more energising than 50 mg of caffeine, though the effect was short-lived.⁠[3]

Is it OK to have coffee in the afternoon?

It depends on you and your bedtime. Caffeine’s mean half-life is about 5 hours,⁠[6] and the authors of a sleep study recommend avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bed.⁠[7]
