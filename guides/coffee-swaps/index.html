What to drink instead of coffee

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

If you’re quitting, herbal teas such as chamomile have no caffeine,[1] and decaf coffee typically has 2 to 15 mg in an 8-fluid-ounce cup.[2] If you’re cutting back, green tea has about 37 mg per 12 fluid ounces, against 113–247 mg for regular brewed coffee.[2]

Caffeine-free

  • Herbal teas. USDA lists 0 mg of caffeine for brewed chamomile tea[1] and for other brewed herbal teas.[3] Peppermint, rooibos and fruit teas are herbal; check the label for blends that include real tea.
  • Hot water with lemon or ginger, if it’s the warm mug you miss.
  • Chicory and mushroom “coffees”. Often sold as coffee alternatives, but some blends include real coffee or tea, so check the label for caffeine.

Very low caffeine

  • Decaf coffee typically has 2 to 15 mg in an 8-fluid-ounce cup.[2] In one study, decaf espresso from a single coffee shop ranged from 3.0 to 15.8 mg per shot.[4] More in our guide to caffeine in decaf.

Lower caffeine, for cutting back

  • Green tea: 37 mg per 12 fluid ounces.[2]
  • Black tea: about 50 mg in a 220 ml cup.[5]
  • Half-caf: half regular coffee and half decaf, so roughly half the caffeine of your usual cup.
  • A smaller size of what you already drink.

For comparison, EFSA gives about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee.[5] See caffeine in drinks for more, caffeine in tea for black, green and matcha, and how to cut back on coffee for a step-by-step approach.

Keep the ritual

For many people, the habit is as much about the moment as the caffeine: the warm mug, the break, the walk to the coffee shop. Keeping the moment and changing what’s in the cup can make a swap easier to stick with. Swapping an energy drink instead? See how to quit energy drinks.

Questions

What can I drink instead of coffee in the morning?

Decaf if you want the taste, with 2 to 15 mg in an 8-fluid-ounce cup,[2] or a herbal tea with no caffeine.[1] Green tea is an option if you’re cutting back rather than quitting.

Do herbal teas have caffeine?

USDA lists 0 mg for brewed chamomile and other brewed herbal teas.[1][3] Check the label for blends that include real tea.

Is green tea a good way to cut back on caffeine?

It has less caffeine than coffee: 37 mg per 12 fluid ounces against 113–247 mg for regular brewed coffee.[2]

Does decaf have caffeine?

A little. The FDA says decaf coffee typically has 2 to 15 mg in an 8-fluid-ounce cup.[2]
