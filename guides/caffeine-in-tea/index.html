How much caffeine is in tea: black, green and matcha?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

EFSA gives about 50 mg of caffeine for a 220 ml cup of black tea,⁠[1] and the FDA gives 71 mg for 12 fluid ounces of black tea and 37 mg for 12 fluid ounces of green tea.⁠[2] Matcha varies a lot: one study of 15 green teas measured 17.11 to 35.01 mg per gram of tea, with ceremonial matcha averaging 31.45 mg per gram.⁠[3]

The sources measure in different ways (a cup, a can-sized serving, 100 g of brewed tea, a gram of dry powder), so compare within a row’s own units rather than across them.

Why one cup of tea differs from the next

A study of black and green tea found that the tea’s variety, growing conditions, processing and leaf size all affect the caffeine, and that how you make it (how much tea and water, how long it steeps, whether it’s in a bag, how much you stir) is a major factor in what ends up in your cup.⁠[9] So a strong, long-brewed mug can be quite different from a quick, weak one.

How much caffeine is in matcha?

Matcha is green tea ground into a powder that you whisk into water. In a study of 15 green teas, ceremonial matcha averaged 31.45 mg of caffeine per gram, culinary matcha 26.29 mg per gram and bagged or loose green tea 23.08 mg per gram of tea.⁠[3] Another study measured much less, 2.21 mg per gram, in the one Japanese matcha powder it tested.⁠[10] Matcha products clearly vary, so how much powder you use, and the label, matter more than the name.

Tea or coffee: which has more caffeine?

Per cup, coffee usually has more. EFSA gives about 50 mg for a 220 ml cup of black tea against about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee.⁠[1] The FDA gives 37 mg for 12 fluid ounces of green tea against 113 to 247 mg for 12 fluid ounces of regular brewed coffee.⁠[2] That’s why many people use tea as a step when they cut back on coffee; for more swaps see coffee swaps and the full caffeine in drinks table.

Does tea affect sleep?

It can. EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg of caffeine may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime.⁠[1] A couple of strong cups can add up to that. Herbal teas such as chamomile have none,⁠[7] so they’re an easy evening swap. See caffeine and sleep.

Questions

Does green tea have less caffeine than coffee?

Usually, per cup. The FDA gives 37 mg for 12 fluid ounces of green tea and 113 to 247 mg for 12 fluid ounces of regular brewed coffee.⁠[2]

How much caffeine is in matcha?

It varies. One study of 15 green teas found ceremonial matcha averaged 31.45 mg of caffeine per gram of powder and culinary matcha 26.29 mg per gram.⁠[3] Check the label and how much powder you use.

Does black tea have more caffeine than green tea?

In the FDA’s figures, yes: 71 mg for 12 fluid ounces of black tea against 37 mg for green tea.⁠[2] How you brew it makes a big difference too.⁠[9]

Does herbal tea have caffeine?

USDA lists 0 mg for brewed chamomile and for other brewed herbal teas.⁠[7]⁠[8] Blends that include real tea leaves do have caffeine, so check the label.
