Caffeine withdrawal headache: how long it lasts and what helps

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

By the International Headache Society’s definition, a caffeine withdrawal headache develops within 24 hours of your last caffeine and resolves on its own within 7 days without more caffeine.⁠[1] In experimental studies, the incidence of headache after stopping caffeine was 50%.⁠[2]

How long a caffeine headache lasts

The International Classification of Headache Disorders describes caffeine-withdrawal headache as a headache developing within 24 hours after regular caffeine use is interrupted, which resolves spontaneously within 7 days in the absence of further consumption.⁠[1]

Research on withdrawal as a whole gives a similar picture: symptoms typically start 12–24 hours after the last caffeine and last 2–9 days.⁠[2]

Who gets it

The ICHD-3 definition applies after more than 200 mg of caffeine a day for more than 2 weeks.⁠[1] But studies show headaches at lower amounts too:

  • In a double-blind study of 62 adults with an average intake of 235 mg a day, 52% had a moderate or severe headache when their caffeine was secretly replaced with a placebo.⁠[3]
  • A review found withdrawal symptoms after stopping doses as low as 100 mg a day.⁠[2]

What may help

The NHS’s general advice for headaches includes drinking plenty of water, trying to relax, not skipping meals and taking paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen.⁠[4] Check with a pharmacist before taking painkillers if you take other medicines or have a health condition.

Caffeine itself relieves the headache: the ICHD-3 criteria include relief within 1 hour of 100 mg of caffeine.⁠[1] That’s one reason some people choose to cut back gradually rather than stop all at once. If tiredness is the harder part for you, see tiredness after quitting caffeine.

When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a GP if you’ve tried treating a headache yourself and it’s not getting better or is getting worse, or if you get headaches regularly.⁠[4]

Get emergency help straight away if a headache started suddenly and is extremely painful, or comes with a seizure, numbness or weakness in the body or face, loss of vision, a head injury in the last 3 months, a rash that doesn’t fade when a glass is rolled over it, or a very high temperature, a stiff neck or sensitivity to bright light.⁠[4] A headache like that may not be caffeine withdrawal.

Questions

How long does a caffeine withdrawal headache last?

By the ICHD-3 definition it resolves within 7 days without more caffeine.⁠[1] Withdrawal symptoms in general typically last 2–9 days.⁠[2]

Will a cup of coffee stop a caffeine headache?

It may. The ICHD-3 criteria include relief within 1 hour of 100 mg of caffeine.⁠[1] If you’re cutting back, a smaller amount rather than none may be part of a gradual plan.

Can I take painkillers for a caffeine withdrawal headache?

The NHS’s general headache advice includes paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen.⁠[4] Check with a pharmacist first if you take other medicines or have a health condition.

How can I avoid a caffeine withdrawal headache?

Some people cut back gradually. In one experiment, people used to 300 mg a day only showed withdrawal once their dose dropped to 100 mg a day or less.⁠[5]
