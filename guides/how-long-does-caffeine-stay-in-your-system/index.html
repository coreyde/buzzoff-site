How long does caffeine stay in your system?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

The mean half-life of caffeine in healthy people is about 5 hours, but it can range from 1.5 to 9.5 hours from person to person (National Academy of Sciences, 2001). With a 5-hour half-life, about half the caffeine from a 2 pm coffee may still be in your body around 7 pm.

What half-life means

Half-life is how long it takes your body to clear half of the caffeine. After each half-life, about half of what was left remains. With the mean half-life of about 5 hours (National Academy of Sciences, 2001), 100 mg of caffeine at 8 am works out roughly like this:

Worked example: 100 mg at 8 am, 5-hour half-life
TimeHalf-lives passedCaffeine left (estimate)
1 pm1about 50 mg
6 pm2about 25 mg
11 pm3about 12.5 mg

This is simple maths, not a measurement. It ignores the time caffeine takes to be absorbed, and your own half-life may be shorter or longer.

How fast caffeine kicks in

Caffeine is rapidly and completely absorbed, with 99 percent absorbed within 45 minutes of drinking it, and peak levels in the blood come between 15 and 120 minutes afterwards (National Academy of Sciences, 2001).

Why it varies so much

The wide range comes from both individual differences and things that change how fast caffeine is broken down, including pregnancy, obesity, oral contraceptives, smoking and altitude (National Academy of Sciences, 2001). Two examples from the same source:

  • Smoking speeds up caffeine metabolism, and stopping smoking returns it to non-smoking rates (National Academy of Sciences, 2001).
  • Oral contraceptives can double caffeine’s half-life (National Academy of Sciences, 2001).

Genes play a part in how caffeine affects you, too: one study linked a variant of the ADORA2A gene to greater susceptibility to caffeine-induced anxiety (Rogers et al., 2010).

What it means for sleep

Because caffeine can stay in your body for hours, an afternoon coffee may still be around at bedtime. EFSA notes that single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime (EFSA). Our guide to caffeine and sleep covers when to stop.

Clearing caffeine isn’t the same as withdrawal

Caffeine leaving your body over hours is different from withdrawal, which typically lasts 2–9 days after you stop (Juliano & Griffiths, 2004). See caffeine withdrawal.

Questions

How long does caffeine last in your body?

Its mean half-life in healthy people is about 5 hours, ranging from 1.5 to 9.5 hours (National Academy of Sciences, 2001). So it takes several hours for most of a cup’s caffeine to clear.

How long does it take for caffeine to kick in?

Peak levels in the blood come between 15 and 120 minutes after you drink it, and 99 percent is absorbed within 45 minutes (National Academy of Sciences, 2001).

Why does caffeine affect some people more than others?

Half-life varies with factors such as pregnancy, oral contraceptives, smoking and individual differences (National Academy of Sciences, 2001), and genes affect how anxious caffeine makes some people (Rogers et al., 2010).

Does caffeine affect sleep even hours later?

It can. EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime (EFSA).
