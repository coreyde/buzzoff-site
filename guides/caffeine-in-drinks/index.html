How much caffeine is in coffee, tea and soda?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

A 200 ml cup of filter coffee has about 90 mg of caffeine, a 220 ml cup of black tea about 50 mg and a 355 ml can of cola about 40 mg, according to EFSA.[1] Real amounts vary a lot by brand, brew and cup size, so check labels where you can.

Why the same coffee can vary so much

A study of coffee-shop drinks found caffeine ranging from 58 to 259 mg per serving across caffeinated coffees, with an average of 188 mg in a 16-oz cup of brewed specialty coffee.[4] The same drink bought from the same outlet on six days in a row ranged from 259 to 564 mg.[4] A lot can differ from cup to cup, which is why tables like the one in Caffeine in common drinks are a guide, not a guarantee.

Energy drinks

The FDA says energy drinks generally have 54 to 328 mg of caffeine per 16 fluid ounces.[2] The label is your best guide for a specific can. If you want to stop, see how to quit energy drinks.

How much caffeine a day is too much?

For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day, about two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee, as an amount not generally associated with negative effects.[2] EFSA says intakes up to 400 mg a day spread through the day don’t raise safety concerns for healthy adults, except pregnant women, and that up to 200 mg a day doesn’t raise safety concerns for the foetus.[1]

Lower amounts can still matter for sleep: EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime.[1] See caffeine and sleep, and for jitters and anxiety, caffeine and anxiety.

Questions

How much caffeine is in a cup of coffee?

EFSA gives about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee and about 80 mg for a 60 ml espresso.[1] The FDA gives 113–247 mg for 12 fluid ounces of regular brewed coffee.[2]

Does tea have more caffeine than coffee?

Not per cup. EFSA gives about 50 mg for a 220 ml cup of black tea against about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee.[1]

How much caffeine is in an energy drink?

EFSA gives about 80 mg for a standard 250 ml can.[1] The FDA says energy drinks generally have 54 to 328 mg per 16 fluid ounces,[2] so check the label.

How much caffeine is safe per day?

For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects.[2] In pregnancy, EFSA’s figure is up to 200 mg a day.[1]
