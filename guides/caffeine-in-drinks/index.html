How much caffeine is in coffee, tea and soda?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

A 200 ml cup of filter coffee has about 90 mg of caffeine, a 220 ml cup of black tea about 50 mg and a 355 ml can of cola about 40 mg, according to EFSA (EFSA). Real amounts vary a lot by brand, brew and cup size, so check labels where you can.

Why the same coffee can vary so much

A study of coffee-shop drinks found caffeine ranging from 58 to 259 mg per serving across caffeinated coffees, with an average of 188 mg in a 16-oz cup of brewed specialty coffee (McCusker et al., 2003). The same drink bought from the same outlet on six days in a row ranged from 259 to 564 mg (McCusker et al., 2003). A lot can differ from cup to cup, which is why tables like the one above are a guide, not a guarantee.

Energy drinks

The FDA says energy drinks generally have 54 to 328 mg of caffeine per 16 fluid ounces (FDA). The label is your best guide for a specific can.

How much caffeine a day is too much?

For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day, about two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee, as an amount not generally associated with negative effects (FDA). EFSA says intakes up to 400 mg a day spread through the day don’t raise safety concerns for healthy adults, except pregnant women, and that up to 200 mg a day doesn’t raise safety concerns for the foetus (EFSA).

Lower amounts can still matter for sleep: EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime (EFSA). See caffeine and sleep.

Questions

How much caffeine is in a cup of coffee?

EFSA gives about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee and about 80 mg for a 60 ml espresso (EFSA). The FDA gives 113–247 mg for 12 fluid ounces of regular brewed coffee (FDA).

Does tea have more caffeine than coffee?

Not per cup. EFSA gives about 50 mg for a 220 ml cup of black tea against about 90 mg for a 200 ml cup of filter coffee (EFSA).

How much caffeine is in an energy drink?

EFSA gives about 80 mg for a standard 250 ml can (EFSA). The FDA says energy drinks generally have 54 to 328 mg per 16 fluid ounces (FDA), so check the label.

How much caffeine is safe per day?

For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects (FDA). In pregnancy, EFSA’s figure is up to 200 mg a day (EFSA).
