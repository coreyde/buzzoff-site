When should you stop drinking caffeine before bed?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

In a study that tracked sleep at home, 400 mg of caffeine taken at bedtime, 3 hours before or even 6 hours before bed significantly disturbed sleep, and the authors recommend avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bedtime.[1] EFSA notes that even 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime.[2]

What the research found

In the study, adults took 400 mg of caffeine or a placebo at bedtime, 3 hours before and 6 hours before going to bed. Their sleep was recorded with sleep diaries and with a validated portable sleep monitor at home.[1]

Caffeine at all three times had significant effects on sleep disturbance compared with the placebo. The authors concluded that caffeine taken 6 hours before bedtime has important disruptive effects on sleep, and that the results support refraining from substantial caffeine use for a minimum of 6 hours before bed.[1] In the discussion, the paper reports that even at 6 hours, caffeine reduced sleep by more than 1 hour.[3]

400 mg is a lot in one go: about what the FDA describes as two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee.[4] Smaller amounts can still matter, as EFSA notes for 100 mg close to bedtime.[2]

Why an afternoon coffee can reach bedtime

Caffeine’s mean half-life in healthy people is about 5 hours, ranging from 1.5 to 9.5 hours.[5] With a 5-hour half-life, about a quarter of a 1 pm coffee’s caffeine may still be in your body at 11 pm. If your half-life is longer, more of it will be. See how long caffeine stays in your system.

Regular caffeine and daytime sleepiness

A review of the research found that regular daily caffeine intake is associated with disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness, and that the risks to sleep and alertness of regular caffeine use are greatly underestimated by both the public and doctors.[6]

Finding your cut-off

  • Count back from bedtime. If you sleep at 11 pm, the study’s 6-hour guide means your last caffeine by about 5 pm. Many people go earlier.
  • Swap the afternoon cup for decaf or a herbal tea. If it’s the afternoon dip you’re fighting, see the afternoon slump without coffee.
  • Remember tea, cola and energy drinks count too; see caffeine in drinks and caffeine in tea.
  • Try a cut-off for a week or two and notice how you sleep.

Questions

How many hours before bed should I stop caffeine?

The authors of a study on caffeine and sleep recommend avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bedtime.[1] Some people need longer, because caffeine’s half-life ranges from 1.5 to 9.5 hours.[5]

Is a small coffee in the afternoon OK for sleep?

It depends on you. EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime.[2]

Does tea affect sleep too?

Tea contains caffeine: EFSA gives about 50 mg for a 220 ml cup of black tea.[2] Herbal teas such as chamomile have none.[7]

Can regular caffeine make you sleepy in the day?

A review found regular daily caffeine intake is associated with disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness.[6]
