Can caffeine make anxiety worse?

By Corey De Ponte, founder of Buzz Off · Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Short answer

For some people, yes: the FDA lists anxiety and jitters among the signs of too much caffeine.[1] A review found caffeine has been shown to increase anxiety when very large amounts are given or in sensitive groups, such as people with anxiety disorders.[2]

Who may be more sensitive?

  • People with panic disorder. A review of placebo-controlled studies found that at doses of 400–750 mg, roughly five cups of coffee, about half of the patients with panic disorder had a panic attack after caffeine and none did after placebo.[3] Caffeine also increased anxiety in healthy adults at those doses, and the authors note little is known about smaller doses.[3]
  • People with certain genes. In a study of 379 people, those with one variant of the ADORA2A gene were more likely to feel anxious after caffeine.[4]
  • People who rarely have caffeine. The same study found regular caffeine users had a smaller anxiety response than people who had little or none, which suggests tolerance builds with frequent use.[4]

The FDA adds that body weight, medications, medical conditions and individual sensitivity all change what “too much” means for each person.[1]

How much caffeine is a lot?

For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day, about two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee, as an amount not generally associated with negative effects.[1] EFSA says single doses up to 200 mg don’t raise safety concerns for healthy adults.[5] These are general figures, not a personal limit: if caffeine leaves you jittery or anxious, your own threshold may be lower. Can cutting back on caffeine help?

Some people who find caffeine makes them anxious choose to cut back or stop, and some say they feel calmer. The research can’t promise that for everyone, and everyone’s different. If you do cut back, the FDA says it’s best to do so gradually if you’re used to caffeine every day.[1] Withdrawal symptoms the research confirmed include irritability, low mood and difficulty concentrating, and they typically last 2–9 days.[6] Our guides on cutting back on coffee and quitting caffeine cover both routes.

When to talk to a doctor

The FDA suggests talking with your health care professional if you have signs of too much caffeine, such as anxiety, jitters, a fast heart rate or heart palpitations.[1] If you have panic attacks or an anxiety condition, or take medication for one, talk to your doctor about caffeine and about any change to how much you have.

Questions

Can caffeine cause anxiety?

In some people, yes. The FDA lists anxiety and jitters among the signs of too much caffeine,[1] and a review found caffeine can increase anxiety at very large amounts or in sensitive groups.[2]

Can caffeine cause panic attacks?

In people with panic disorder, studies found that 400–750 mg of caffeine triggered a panic attack in about half of patients, and placebo in none.[3] Talk to your doctor if you have panic attacks.

Why does coffee make me anxious when it doesn’t affect others?

People differ. One study linked a variant of the ADORA2A gene to more anxiety after caffeine, and found regular users had a smaller anxiety response than people who rarely have it.[4]

How much caffeine is too much if I have anxiety?

There’s no single figure. The FDA’s 400 mg a day is for most healthy adults,[1] and some people feel effects at less. Ask your doctor what’s right for you.
