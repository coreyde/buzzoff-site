Caffeine that may be left at bedtime
Half-lifeAt bedtime

Estimate only, everyone’s different. Caffeine’s half-life ranges from 1.5 to 9.5 hours from person to person. It varies with individual differences and with things like pregnancy, obesity, oral contraceptives, smoking and altitude (National Academy of Sciences, 2001).

What this means for sleep

In a study that tracked sleep at home, the authors recommend avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bedtime (Drake et al., 2013). EFSA notes that even 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime (EFSA). Our guide to caffeine and sleep has more.

How this is worked out

Half-life is how long it takes your body to clear half of the caffeine. The mean half-life in healthy people is about 5 hours, with a range of 1.5 to 9.5 hours (National Academy of Sciences, 2001). The chart draws all three: the average as a solid line, and the fastest and slowest in that research range as dashed lines, so you can see how much the answer can change.

Caffeine is rapidly absorbed, with 99 percent absorbed within 45 minutes, and peak levels in the blood come between 15 and 120 minutes after you drink it (National Academy of Sciences, 2001). The tool models absorption so that 99 percent is in within 45 minutes, which puts the peak about 38 minutes after a drink.

The model is a standard simplified one (one compartment, first-order absorption), added up over your drinks. It gives the amount of caffeine in your body, not a blood level, so it doesn’t need your weight. It can’t know your own half-life, so treat it as a rough guide.
