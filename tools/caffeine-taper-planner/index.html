Why step down gradually

In one controlled experiment, people used to 300 mg of caffeine a day were switched to lower doses, and withdrawal only showed up once the dose fell to 100 mg a day or less.[1] The same research found withdrawal got stronger as the usual daily dose went up. Our guide on how to quit caffeine compares cutting back with stopping all at once.

How this is worked out

  • Week 1, track and learn: your target is what you have now.
  • Weeks 2 to 6: each week’s target is 15 percent lower than the week before, rounded to the nearest mg.
  • Floor: a target never goes under 25 mg.
  • Week 6, maintain or adjust: keep going at that level, or change your plan.

These are Buzz Off’s own plan rules, not medical guidance. “About” counts a cup of brewed coffee as 95 mg, as the app does: USDA FoodData Central lists 40 mg of caffeine per 100 g of brewed coffee, which is about 95 mg in an 8 fl oz (237 g) cup.[2]
