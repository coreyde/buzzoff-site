Estimate only, everyone’s different. Real caffeine varies a lot by brand, brew and cup size. One study found the same coffee bought from the same shop on different days ranged from 259 to 564 mg (McCusker et al., 2003), so check labels where you can.

What your total means

These figures are context, not a target. For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 mg a day, about two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee, as an amount not generally associated with negative effects (FDA). EFSA says intakes up to 400 mg a day spread through the day don’t raise safety concerns for healthy adults, except pregnant women, and that up to 200 mg a day doesn’t raise safety concerns for the foetus (EFSA).

Lower amounts can still matter for sleep: EFSA notes single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, particularly close to bedtime (EFSA). Our wear-off calculator shows how much may still be in your body at bedtime.

How this is worked out

The total is each drink’s caffeine times how many you had, added up. Each drink starts from the figure below, quoted as its source gives it, so serving sizes differ. EFSA notes its figures are approximate because caffeine content and portion sizes vary within and between countries (EFSA).
